# Healthcare Quality Improvement

**Practical tools, methods, and resources to improve the quality and safety of healthcare services.**

## About This Repository

This repository provides practical, adaptable resources to support healthcare quality improvement (QI), patient safety, clinical governance, and evidence-informed decision-making.

It is designed for healthcare professionals, facility managers, public health practitioners, researchers, quality improvement teams, and policymakers working to make healthcare safer, more effective, timely, people-centred, equitable, integrated, and efficient.

The resources are intended to support real-world improvement work in hospitals, primary healthcare facilities, community health programmes, and health systems, including resource-constrained settings.

## Objectives

- Translate established quality improvement methods into practical tools.
- Support teams in identifying quality gaps and testing changes.
- Promote the use of data to understand performance and guide improvement.
- Strengthen patient safety, learning, and continuous improvement.
- Encourage patient-centred and equitable healthcare.
- Share reusable templates and learning resources that can be adapted to local contexts.

## Core Frameworks

This repository draws on established approaches and guidance, including:

- **WHO quality health services guidance:** planning, implementing, and monitoring quality improvement.
- **IHI Model for Improvement:** setting aims, selecting measures, and testing changes.
- **Plan–Do–Study–Act (PDSA):** learning through small-scale, iterative tests of change.
- **Measurement for improvement:** using process, outcome, and balancing measures to understand whether changes lead to improvement.
- **Patient safety and learning systems:** identifying risks, learning from incidents, and reducing preventable harm.
- **Equity-oriented quality improvement:** examining whether improvements benefit different population groups.

Resources will be added progressively, with links to relevant evidence and guidance wherever possible.

## Planned Resources

### 1. Model for Improvement

Practical resources to help teams define an improvement project:

- Writing a clear and measurable aim statement.
- Answering the three Model for Improvement questions.
- Identifying change ideas.
- Developing an initial improvement plan.
- Engaging relevant staff and stakeholders.

### 2. PDSA Worksheets

Templates to support structured testing and learning:

- Plan–Do–Study–Act worksheets.
- Small-scale tests of change.
- Documentation of predictions and observations.
- Reflection on lessons learned.
- Planning the next test based on evidence.

### 3. Measurement and Run Charts

Tools to help teams understand whether their changes are producing improvement:

- Outcome, process, and balancing measure definitions.
- Indicator reference sheets.
- Data collection plans.
- Run chart templates.
- Basic guidance on interpreting trends over time.

Each indicator should have a clearly defined numerator, denominator where applicable, data source, measurement frequency, and responsible person.

### 4. Root Cause Analysis and Corrective Action

Resources to support systematic learning from quality problems:

- Problem statements.
- Cause-and-effect analysis.
- Five Whys worksheets.
- Corrective and preventive action planning.
- Follow-up and effectiveness checks.

These tools should support a systems-based, learning-oriented approach rather than automatically assigning blame to individuals.

### 5. Practical Case Studies

Case studies will demonstrate how improvement methods can be applied to real healthcare challenges, such as:

- Improving patient waiting times.
- Strengthening hand hygiene practices.
- Reducing missed appointments.
- Improving referral and follow-up processes.
- Enhancing medication safety.
- Improving patient experience.
- Strengthening continuity of care in primary healthcare.

Examples will distinguish between planned activities, measured results, and lessons learned. Results will not be presented as established evidence unless supported by appropriate data.

## How to Use These Resources

1. Identify a specific quality or safety problem in your setting.
2. Understand the current process and establish a baseline where feasible.
3. Develop a clear, measurable aim.
4. Select a small number of meaningful measures.
5. Involve patients, families, and relevant healthcare professionals as appropriate.
6. Test a change on a small scale using PDSA cycles.
7. Review the data and feedback.
8. Adapt the intervention, test again, and document learning.
9. Assess sustainability and whether the improvement can be spread to other settings.

Adapt each tool to local clinical guidelines, policies, resources, and patient needs before implementation.

- Templates should be adapted to the local context and reviewed by appropriate clinical and quality leads.
- Improvement findings should be interpreted in light of data quality, study design, implementation context, and potential unintended consequences.
- Patient confidentiality and data protection must be maintained at all times.
- Do not upload identifiable patient information, confidential organisational records, or materials that you do not have permission to share.
- Cite the original sources of frameworks and guidance when using or adapting them.
